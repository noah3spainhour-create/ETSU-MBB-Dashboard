Exercise plans

For each player: four squat and four hinge variations picked for his jumper type — to keep him performing or to fix what's holding him back — plus single-leg work aimed at his weaker side when his legs are more than 10% apart. Adjust for training history and any injury status.

Readiness

A 1–10 score from each player's jump performance compared with his own baseline (the average of up to four earlier clean tests). It weights RSI-modified 70% and jump height 30%.

Scale

1–4 Fatigued · cut plyo volume5–7 Monitor · normal session, cap intensity8–10 Ready · full session

7 means jumping at his normal level. Each point is about a 2.5% change in combined jump performance. No score is given on a player's first test or on a test where his bodyweight reading was off.

What the numbers mean

Jump height (Imp-Mom) is height from takeoff velocity, worked out from the force trace and bodyweight. A bodyweight reading that's too low makes it read too high.
RSI-modified is jump height divided by time to takeoff — how much jump a player gets per second of effort.
Countermovement depth is how far the hips drop. Eccentric peak velocity is how fast he drops into it.
Asymmetry is the percent difference between legs and the side that's higher: decel is braking on the way down, concentric is the push up, landing is peak force coming back down.
Bodyweight check: a test is flagged when his bodyweight is 4+ kg off the median of all his tests. Flagged tests aren't used as a comparison point.
